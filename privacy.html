Privacy

Privacy policy

Your images stay yours. PixelShrink is built to do one job without collecting anything about you or your files.

Last updated: 1 November 2026

The short version: images are read and compressed in your browser. They are never uploaded. There are no accounts, no analytics, no ads, and no third-party scripts.

What we do not collect

  • Your images, filenames, or image metadata.
  • An account, email address, or profile.
  • Analytics, advertising identifiers, or cross-site tracking data.
  • Compression history or settings. Nothing is saved between visits.

What happens on your device

When you add an image, the browser reads the file directly from your device. PixelShrink draws it to a local canvas, encodes the result in memory, and offers it back to you as a download. The image data does not pass through a server at any point.

Network requests

The page loads only files from this site: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, icons, and the web app manifest. There are no fonts, scripts, or styles from third-party domains. The app contains no upload or fetch code, and its Content Security Policy sets connect-src 'none' for the page.

The service worker may fetch this site's own files so the tool can work offline. It does not cache or transmit your images.

Cookies and local storage

PixelShrink does not set cookies and does not use localStorage or sessionStorage. A service worker cache may hold this site's app files on your device so it can open offline; you can remove it at any time by clearing site data in your browser.

Hosting

This site is served by GitHub Pages behind Cloudflare. Those providers may process standard request information, such as an IP address and user agent, as part of normal hosting and security operations. PixelShrink does not have an analytics system and does not receive a report of your activity from those providers. See the GitHub privacy statement and the Cloudflare privacy policy.

Your choices

Because there is no account and no stored profile, there is nothing to delete on our side. You can clear the service worker cache and any downloaded files through your browser and file system.

Contact

Questions or bug reports can be opened in the project issue tracker. For a security-sensitive report, use GitHub's private vulnerability reporting for the repository if it is available, and avoid posting sensitive details publicly.

Changes

If this policy changes, the update date at the top of this page will change. The site's source and history are public on GitHub.
